Non-invasive by design

Net Watcher only uses the questions every device on a network already answers. Nothing is installed on the devices, and no traffic is read.

  1. Discover. A fresh ARP request and a ping to every address in the subnet. Phones that ignore ping still have to answer ARP.
  2. Identify. The manufacturer comes from the public IEEE registry; names from NetBIOS, mDNS/Bonjour and the router's DNS; the device type from a few well-known service ports.
  3. Compare. Each device is matched against your registry of approved devices and reserved IPs.
  4. Alert. Unknown devices, conflicts, critical devices going down and internet outages raise a Windows notification and are logged.
Use it responsibly. Run Net Watcher only on networks you own or administer, and let the people using the network know devices are inventoried, as your company policy and privacy law (such as LGPD or GDPR) require.

Questions

Does it slow down the network?

No. A full scan of a 254-address network takes about ten seconds and sends a few small packets per address. Health checks send a handful of pings per minute.

Why do some phones show "Private MAC"?

Modern iPhones and Android phones use a random MAC address per Wi-Fi network for privacy, so there is no manufacturer to look up and the address may change. For staff phones, turn off "Private Wi-Fi address" for the office network so they stay recognized.

Can it block a device?

No, on purpose. Blocking from a PC would mean interfering with network traffic. Use your router's MAC filter, or give visitors a separate guest Wi-Fi.

Can it tell which switch port a device is plugged into?

Not yet. That needs a managed switch with SNMP. It is on the roadmap.

Where is my data stored?

Only on your PC, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\NetWatcher, with an automatic weekly backup. Uninstalling keeps it; delete the folder to remove everything.

Does it need to stay open?

Closing the window keeps it running in the system tray, and it can start automatically when you sign in to Windows (Settings → Start with Windows).
